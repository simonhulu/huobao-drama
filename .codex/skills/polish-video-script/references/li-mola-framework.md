# Li Mola Viral-content Framework

Use this reference to diagnose a topic, contract, opening, stage draft, or final script. Treat it
as a decision framework, not a collection of surface formulas.

## 1. Structure Before Topic

A topic sets the reachable audience ceiling. Structure determines how much of that audience keeps
watching. A narrow topic cannot be made universally broad by editing, but a broad topic can still
fail if the structure has no forward pull.

Build around unanswered meaning, not chronology. Start from the most revealing, costly, strange,
or emotionally charged point. Each stage must change the audience's answer-state.

## 2. The Hole

A hook is an information gap that the audience can feel and name. It is not a question mark,
conflict phrase, or stock opening.

The test:

1. After the opening, can the viewer say in one sentence what information is missing?
2. Is the missing information personally, emotionally, or socially worth obtaining?
3. Is the promised wait proportional to the eventual answer?
4. Does the opening still work without the title and cover?

Every script has one `main_hole`. A subhole is legal only if it does one of these to the main hole:

- `shrink`: eliminate possibilities;
- `deepen`: reveal that the answer is more complex;
- `reverse`: overturn the current interpretation;
- `raise_stakes`: make the cost of the answer matter more;
- `pay`: deliver part or all of the promised value.

Unrelated suspense is a retention trick, not structure. Delete it.

## 3. Promise And Payment

The opening promise and ending payoff must match on three axes:

- value currency: cognition, emotion, or social currency;
- subject: the same person, event, mechanism, or question;
- scope: do not promise a dynasty and pay one isolated decision.

Historical/philosophical content can promise:

- a usable judgment or cognitive reversal;
- a complete emotional experience;
- a story the viewer can retell.

An open ending is legal only when the real answer is genuinely unknown and the script still pays
with known evidence, competing explanations, an investigation boundary, or a useful judgment.

## 4. 山海墓色

Choose one primary driver and at most one secondary driver. These are causal design choices, not
labels to attach after writing.

### 山: costly opposition

The audience watches a person or force push against a real limit. Name both sides, the cost, and
how pressure escalates. Conflict is efficient, but only this driver requires it.

### 海: belief-changing exploration

Each layer of evidence changes the current answer. A list of increasingly obscure facts is not
exploration. The endpoint must be more consequential than the entry point.

### 墓: real forbidden boundary

The attraction comes from crossing a genuine visibility, access, rule, or status boundary. Record
proof of the boundary. Public encyclopedia knowledge cannot be repackaged as “sealed for 300 years.”

### 色: consumed desire or emotion

The emotion, beauty, appetite, healing, outrage, longing, or release is itself the product the
viewer came to consume. Emotion used merely as a tone does not make 色 the primary driver.

## 5. Wide Hook, Vertical Payment

Use a shared human instinct to earn entry: uncertainty, danger, forbidden access, desire, loss,
justice, or identity. Pay it with vertical material: evidence, professional access, creator
experience, or a judgment that a generic summary cannot supply.

Do not broaden by fabricating stakes. Do not stay vertical by speaking only to insiders.

## 6. Judgmental Altruism

Public facts are replaceable. Creator value appears in choices:

- why this topic matters now;
- which fact bears the argument;
- what interpretation the creator accepts;
- what alternative they reject and why;
- what opposition they are willing to attract.

AI can propose a judgment based on public evidence. It cannot turn that proposal into `我认为` or
invent a private experience. The creator must explicitly edit or adopt the judgment.

## 7. Emotion And Logic

Emotion can outrank exhaustive explanation, but not truth or causal intelligibility. Valid omission
lets the audience infer a nonessential bridge. Invalid omission removes the premise needed to
understand the promised result or makes the viewer feel deceived.

Use the smallest causal chain needed for the intended emotion. Never use “emotion wins” to excuse
false dates, motives, quotations, or an unpaid promise.

## 8. Stage Diagnostics

For every stage answer:

1. What does the audience believe before it?
2. What do they believe after it?
3. Which fact or emotional event causes that change?
4. What does this do to the main hole?
5. Which subhole is opened or paid?
6. What must remain unpaid for the next stage?

If belief does not change, the stage is background or repetition. Compress, merge, or delete it.

## 9. Opening Diagnostics

An effective opening may be a declaration, abnormal result, action, image, judgment, or direct
promise. It does not need a visible opponent.

Reject:

- generic questions such as `你有没有想过`;
- “many people don't know” without a precise missing fact;
- fake secrecy;
- title-dependent fragments;
- a promise that the available evidence cannot pay;
- multiple unrelated mysteries.

Prefer a clear abnormality plus a bounded promise. The audience should spontaneously generate the
question rather than being ordered to feel curious.

## 10. Final Diagnosis

A strong script combines:

```text
universal instinct structure
+ reliable new material
+ creator-owned judgment
+ honest promise/payment
+ exact stage-to-stage belief movement
```

Do not predict “will go viral.” Report which conditions are strong, weak, or unproven.
